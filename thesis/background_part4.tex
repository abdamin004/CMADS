\section{Analysis and Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This section synthesises the findings from the 22 reviewed papers into four overarching
themes, identifies the research gaps that collectively define the open problem space this
thesis enters, and provides a comparative summary table of the full literature
corpus.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Key Findings}
\label{subsec:findings}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The following four findings emerge consistently across the reviewed literature and
together establish the theoretical and empirical foundation upon which this thesis is
built.  Each finding is supported by multiple independent papers and has direct
implications for the system design decisions described in
Chapter~3.

\subsubsection*{Dominance of Multi-Agent Approaches Over Single Models}

The most consistent finding across all 22 reviewed papers is that multi-agent LLM
frameworks outperform single-model baselines on medical tasks.  This performance
advantage holds across all evaluated domains --- medical reasoning
benchmarks~\cite{Tang2024MedAgents}, clinical
diagnostics~\cite{Kim2024MDAgents,Zhou2024ZODIAC,Yan2024ClinicalLab},
pharmacovigilance~\cite{Choi2024MALADE}, emergency
triage~\cite{Lu2024TriageAgent}, rare disease
diagnosis~\cite{Chen2024RareAgents}, and inpatient pathway
management~\cite{Chen2025MAP}.  The evidence base is now sufficiently consistent and
broad for the superiority of multi-agent approaches in clinical AI to be considered an
established finding~\cite{MAC2025npj}.

\subsubsection*{Coordination Architecture as the Primary Design Variable}

A second major finding, established most directly by
Bedi~et~al.~\cite{Bedi2025OptParadox} and corroborated by
Chen~et~al.~\cite{Chen2025MedSentry} and Wang~et~al.~\cite{Wang2025Catfish}, is that
the coordination architecture of a multi-agent system is a stronger determinant of
system performance than the quality of individual component models.  This finding
redirects the design effort of clinical AI systems away from individual model selection
and toward workflow architecture as the primary engineering
challenge~\cite{Bedi2025OptParadox}.

\subsubsection*{Conflict Resolution as a Structural Necessity}

Fan~et~al.~\cite{Fan2024AIHospital} demonstrated that removing conflict resolution
from a multi-agent clinical system caused a statistically significant reduction in
diagnostic accuracy.  Wang~et~al.~\cite{Wang2025Catfish} identified silent agreement as
a systematic failure mode.  Zhao~et~al.~\cite{Zhao2025LayeredDebate} and
Chen~et~al.~\cite{Chen2025MDTeamGPT} demonstrated that structured debate with
adversarial evidence exchange consistently outperforms direct consensus on ambiguous
clinical presentations~\cite{Zhao2025LayeredDebate}.

\subsubsection*{Safety Is Architecturally Determined}

Chen~et~al.~\cite{Chen2025MedSentry} provided the clearest evidence that clinical safety
in a multi-agent system is architecturally determined: the topology of agent
communication is a primary safety variable that cannot be compensated by individual
agent safety properties.  Kim~et~al.~\cite{Kim2025TAO} demonstrated that a tiered
hierarchical architecture designed around safety objectives achieves measurably better
safety outcomes than a system designed around accuracy
alone~\cite{Kim2025TAO}.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Research Gaps Identified}
\label{subsec:gaps}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Despite the substantial progress documented above, the reviewed literature reveals a
consistent set of limitations that define the open research space this thesis
enters.  The following four gaps are each
supported by the evidence reviewed in the preceding sections and correspond directly to
the four challenges articulated in the Problem Statement
(Section~\ref{sec:problem}).

First, no reviewed paper proposes a solution to the Optimisation
Paradox~\cite{Bedi2025OptParadox}: the identification of the coordination quality
problem is well-established, but the design of a workflow architecture that resolves it
in a generalisable way remains an open problem.  Second, with the exception of
ClinicalLab~\cite{Yan2024ClinicalLab} and ZODIAC~\cite{Zhou2024ZODIAC}, the vast
majority of evaluated frameworks are tested on structured benchmarks rather than
real-world clinical deployments with live patient data, limiting the external validity
of performance claims~\cite{Xiang2025CDRAgent}.  Third, conflict resolution mechanisms
across reviewed papers are either absent, rule-based, or evaluated only on specific
narrow tasks --- no framework provides a generalisable, adaptive conflict resolution
mechanism for open-ended clinical decisioning under
uncertainty~\cite{Wang2025Catfish,Zhao2025LayeredDebate}.  Fourth, the safety dimension
is addressed by only three of 22 reviewed
papers~\cite{Kim2025TAO,Wu2024EHRFlow,Chen2025MedSentry}, and systematic evaluation of
the accuracy--safety trade-off incurred by safety mechanisms is absent from all reviewed
works~\cite{Chen2025MedSentry}.

This thesis addresses these gaps through the design, implementation, and evaluation of a
multi-agent clinical decisioning system with an explicit focus on coordination
architecture quality, adaptive conflict resolution, and built-in error-recovery
mechanisms~\cite{Bedi2025OptParadox,Wu2024EHRFlow}.
