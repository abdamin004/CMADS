\section{Analysis and Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The reviewed papers point to a small number of common lessons. This final section
summarises those lessons, explains the gaps that remain, and gives a comparative
table of the 22 papers.

\FloatBarrier
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Key Findings}
\label{subsec:findings}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Four findings appear repeatedly in the reviewed papers. These findings explain why
this thesis uses a multi-agent workflow with review, memory, and explicit
evaluation.

\subsubsection*{Multi-Agent Systems Usually Perform Better Than Single Models}

Most reviewed papers report that multi-agent systems outperform single-model
baselines on their tested tasks. This appears across medical reasoning
benchmarks~\cite{Tang2024MedAgents}, clinical diagnosis
\cite{Kim2024MDAgents,Zhou2024ZODIAC,Yan2024ClinicalLab}, pharmacovigilance
\cite{Choi2024MALADE}, emergency triage~\cite{Lu2024TriageAgent},
rare-disease diagnosis~\cite{Chen2024RareAgents}, and inpatient pathway
management~\cite{Chen2025MAP}. This does not mean every multi-agent design is
good, but it does show that splitting clinical work across roles can be useful.

\subsubsection*{Coordination Is as Important as Model Choice}

Bedi et al. show that choosing the best individual component for each stage does
not guarantee the best final system \cite{Bedi2025OptParadox}. Chen et al. and
Wang et al. support the same point from safety and debate perspectives
\cite{Chen2025MedSentry,Wang2025Catfish}. The practical lesson is that model
choice matters, but agent handoffs, routing, and review structure matter as much
or more.

\subsubsection*{Conflict Resolution Must Be Designed}

Clinical agents should not automatically agree with each other. Fan et al. found
that removing dispute resolution reduced diagnostic accuracy
\cite{Fan2024AIHospital}. Wang et al. describe silent agreement, where agents
appear to collaborate but do not really challenge the first answer
\cite{Wang2025Catfish}. Zhao et al. and Chen et al. show that structured debate
can improve difficult diagnostic tasks
\cite{Zhao2025LayeredDebate,Chen2025MDTeamGPT}.

\subsubsection*{Safety Depends on the Workflow}

Safety is not only a property of the base model. MedSentry shows that the
communication topology changes how vulnerable the system is to a compromised
agent \cite{Chen2025MedSentry}. TAO shows that a workflow designed around risk
and escalation can improve safety outcomes \cite{Kim2025TAO}. This supports the
use of clear stage boundaries and review points in the thesis system.

\paragraph{Closest contemporary evaluation shapes.}
Two recent works deserve mention here because their evaluation
protocols are the closest published comparators to the present thesis.
\textbf{AMIE} \cite{Tu2024AMIE} is a conversational diagnostic system
evaluated through OSCE-style consultations against primary-care
physicians, reporting differential-diagnosis accuracy and history-taking
quality across patient-actor scenarios. AMIE's evaluation maps onto
this thesis's DIRECT and Found metrics; the present work differs in
running on full Synthea-derived EHR records rather than scripted
OSCE encounters, and in evaluating an end-to-end multi-agent pipeline
with persistent memory and treatment planning rather than a
single-agent conversational model. \textbf{AgentClinic}
\cite{Schmidgall2024AgentClinic} is a multimodal benchmark of LLM
agents in simulated clinical environments, reporting diagnostic
accuracy across several LLM backbones with a clinician baseline.
AgentClinic provides a structured benchmark rather than a deployed
pipeline; the present work is closer to a deployment shape, running
the same model under controlled patient identity and reporting paired
McNemar tests on both memory and orchestration A/B comparisons.
Together AMIE and AgentClinic are the closest published evaluation
shapes to the protocol used in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}; neither
ships the combination of end-to-end EHR ingestion, non-destructive
Reviewer-Refiner, CoALA-style four-tier memory, and a doctor-facing
inspection interface that this thesis evaluates as one system.

\paragraph{Synthea as a data substrate for diagnostic AI.}
The data source itself deserves a brief comment. The most directly
comparable contemporary work that evaluates a diagnostic AI system on
Synthea-generated patients is the neural-network telemedicine study
of Rahman and Emdad \cite{Rahman2025SyntheaNN}, which generates
100,000 synthetic encounters with Synthea and trains a family of
tabular classifiers (MLP, deep MLP, TabNet, and a custom PyTorch
network) to detect metabolic, oncology, and allergy/immunology
conditions; their reported F1 drops to 0.581 when telemedicine
context is limited. The two designs operate at opposite ends of the
design space: their work treats Synthea as a large statistical
training cohort for tabular classifiers under feature drop-out,
while the present work treats a smaller, deeply-annotated Synthea
cohort as a substrate for multi-agent LLM differential diagnosis
with persistent memory and treatment planning. To the best of the
literature search reported in Section~\ref{sec:search}, no prior
multi-agent LLM diagnostic pipeline has been evaluated end-to-end on
Synthea-derived patient records; the present thesis is the first to
do so in this configuration.

\subsection{Research Gaps Identified}
\label{subsec:gaps}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The reviewed papers leave four gaps that are relevant to this thesis. First, the
Optimisation Paradox is clearly identified, but there is still a need to design
workflows that handle this problem in practice \cite{Bedi2025OptParadox}.
Second, many systems are still evaluated on structured benchmarks rather than
full patient workflows, although ClinicalLab and ZODIAC move closer to real
deployment \cite{Yan2024ClinicalLab,Zhou2024ZODIAC}. Third, conflict resolution
is often missing, narrow, or tested only on a special task
\cite{Wang2025Catfish,Zhao2025LayeredDebate}. Fourth, safety and reliability are
still under-studied; only a few reviewed papers focus on them directly
\cite{Kim2025TAO,Wu2024EHRFlow,Chen2025MedSentry}. A fifth gap, identified in
Section~\ref{subsec:memory_state}, is that memory-augmented clinical multi-agent
systems are not currently evaluated under a protocol that separates the cohort
used to populate the memory store from the cohort used to test the with-memory
configuration, so apparent gains can be confounded with same-cohort
contamination.

Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology} responds to each of these gaps directly. The
end-to-end coordination problem is addressed by a LangGraph
\texttt{StateGraph} with six clinical stages plus one memory-consolidation
stage that wires the full pipeline from EHR analysis through treatment
planning and Stage~7 memory consolidation into a single typed graph
(Section~\ref{sec:mas_framework}). The conflict-resolution gap is addressed
by the non-destructive Reviewer-Refiner pattern that lets the Clinical
Reviewer challenge the Diagnostic Reasoning agent while preserving the
original output for audit (Section~\ref{sec:agent_refiner}). The memory gap
is addressed by the four-tier shared-memory subsystem of
Section~\ref{sec:shared_memory}, which instantiates the CoALA taxonomy of
Section~\ref{subsec:memory_state}. The evaluation gap is addressed by the
LLM-as-judge framework of Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}, which compares
free-text differentials against hidden Synthea ground truth on a three-label
scale. The leakage-control gap is addressed more cautiously in
Chapter~\ref{chap:results} by reporting the memory comparison across all
available runs for each memory setting and making the cohort-leakage risk
explicit. This does not fully solve leakage, but it prevents the thesis from
claiming that memory improves the system when the observed pattern is instead
broader differentials and fewer misses. The remaining gap ---
safety and reliability of clinical MAS under adversarial conditions --- is
acknowledged but is outside the scope of this thesis and is returned to as
future work in Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion}.
